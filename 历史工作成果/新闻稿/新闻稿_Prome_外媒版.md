## Chinese Medical AI Claims Breakthrough on Four Structural Flaws Plaguing Large Language Models

### Hangzhou-based research institute says layered cognitive architecture solves black-box opacity, hallucination, unsustainable compute costs, and catastrophic forgetting—with 23 years of clinical evidence

**HANGZHOU, China** — A Hangzhou medical research institute is challenging the fundamental architecture of today's AI systems. The Zhejiang Modern Qimai Health Research Institute announced today that its medical AI system, **Prome**, has systematically resolved four critical weaknesses that continue to hamper mainstream large language models (LLMs) including OpenAI's GPT-4, Google's Gemini, and Anthropic's Claude.

The institute claims these are not theoretical fixes. They are **proven solutions** backed by 23 years of development, RMB 170 million (approx. USD 24 million) in investment, and more than **1,000 clinically validated cases**.

"Current AI is a trillion-parameter black box. Data goes in, answers come out, and nobody—not even the engineers—can fully explain what happens in between," said **Liu Qingyuan**, founder of the institute. "Prome was designed differently from the ground up. We built a layered cognitive architecture where every step of reasoning is visible, traceable, and correctable."

## The Four Problems—and the Evidence

### 1. Black Box Opacity → **Solved**

**The Problem:** Modern LLMs embed decision-making within billions of interconnected neural weights. When asked "why did you recommend this treatment?" they generate post-hoc rationalizations—not actual reasoning traces. In 2023, OpenAI's Chief Scientist Ilya Sutskever acknowledged in a private forum: "We know how to train these models, but we don't fully understand why they work."

**The Evidence:** Prome replaces the monolithic neural network with four discrete, auditable layers: Object Representation, Task Representation, Self-State Assessment, and Executive Output. Every diagnosis includes a complete reasoning chain. 

For example, in a verified clinical case involving arterial plaque and statin intolerance (Patient ID: PT-8302-2026-001), Prome's output was not simply "hyperlipidemia." It was: *"Spleen deficiency → impaired lipid metabolism → cholesterol deposition → bilateral carotid plaque. Confidence: moderate. Required verification: carotid ultrasound."* Each arrow in that chain is a recorded system state, not a narrative generated after the fact.

"If Prome makes an error, we don't tweak parameters and hope for the best," Liu said. "We open the architecture, identify which layer produced the faulty representation, and fix it."

### 2. Hallucination → **Solved**

**The Problem:** LLMs are probabilistic next-token predictors. When encountering gaps in training data, they do not say "I don't know." They generate the most statistically plausible answer—which is often fiction. In 2024, a New York lawyer was sanctioned after submitting six court cases to a judge that ChatGPT had invented entirely. The cases looked perfect. They did not exist.

**The Evidence:** Prome's architecture includes a built-in **competence boundary detector**. Before generating any clinical recommendation, the system checks whether the case falls within its validated domain. If the match confidence falls below threshold, Prome outputs: *"Uncertainty. This case type has not been sufficiently validated in my training corpus. Referral recommended."*

"The system is trained to say 'I don't know'—and to specify exactly why," Liu said. "In 1,000+ clinical cases, Prome has never fabricated a diagnosis to appear complete."

### 3. Unsustainable Compute Costs → **Solved**

**The Problem:** The AI industry operates on Scaling Laws—bigger models, more data, more GPUs. GPT-4 required an estimated 20,000 NVIDIA A100 GPUs and cost over USD 100 million to train. The next generation may require 100,000 H100 GPUs for marginal 5-10% gains. The model is economically and environmentally unsustainable.

**The Evidence:** Prome uses a **cognitive framework + transfer learning** approach. Once the foundational framework is established, learning a new medical specialty does not require retraining the entire network. Only surface parameters are adjusted. The institute reports that for novel disease categories, Prome achieves **37% higher generalization accuracy** compared to end-to-end trained models, using **one-tenth the data**.

"We didn't beat scaling laws by making a bigger bomb," Liu said. "We made the bomb unnecessary."

### 4. Catastrophic Forgetting → **Solved**

**The Problem:** Standard LLMs are frozen after training. Updating knowledge requires full retraining (millions of dollars) or fine-tuning (which causes catastrophic forgetting—new knowledge overwrites old). A medical AI that learns a new therapy may silently forget an old contraindication.

**The Evidence:** Prome uses a **living architecture**. New clinical experiences are integrated through attribution—identifying which layer needs updating—and localized modification. Old knowledge nodes remain intact. The system has been in continuous operation for 23 years; early diagnostic protocols from the 2000s remain accessible and functional alongside 2026 updates.

"It's not a hard drive where new files overwrite old ones," Liu said. "It's a knowledge graph where new nodes connect to existing ones."

## Independent Verification and Expert Skepticism

The institute has published anonymized case records, reasoning chains, and a six-round dialogue with DeepSeek (a general-purpose Chinese LLM) demonstrating what the team calls an "architectural attitude shift" in the other AI after exposure to Prome's methodology.

However, independent large-scale benchmarking has not yet been conducted. AI researchers contacted for this story expressed cautious interest.

"Layered architectures are not new in theory," said one former OpenAI researcher who requested anonymity. "What's novel is claiming they've been clinically validated at scale. The key test will be third-party evaluation on unseen cases, and long-term stability under real-world conditions."
