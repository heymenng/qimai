Dear Editors,

We wish to submit an original case report entitled "**Liquefaction and Detoxification in Qimai Medicine for Breast Cancer with Liver Metastasis: A 555-Session Follow-up Case Report**" for consideration of publication in the *Journal of Ethnopharmacology*.

**Significance and Novelty**

This case report documents a 65-year-old female patient with left breast cancer and liver metastasis who underwent 555 sessions of Qimai Medicine therapy over 2 years and 3 months, in conjunction with standard endocrine therapy (letrozole) and targeted therapy (dalpiciclib). The key findings include:

1. **Tumor marker normalization**: Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) decreased from 11.3 ng/mL (December 2024) to 2.05-2.16 ng/mL (November-December 2025), returning to the normal range (<5.00 ng/mL).

2. **Radiological improvement**: Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) showed that the liver segment IV metastasis became "indistinct" after treatment, and a previously identified nodule at the inferior liver margin was "no longer clearly visible." Ultrasound confirmed "no obvious abnormal mass echoes" in the liver.

3. **Unique "liquefaction detoxification" phenomenon**: The patient exhibited a characteristic detoxification response including yellow fluid discharge from the umbilicus (persisting for 9 months), ulcerative drainage at the tumor site, and desquamation of the hands—phenomena rarely documented in modern medical literature.

**Why Journal of Ethnopharmacology?**

We believe this case is particularly suitable for your journal because:
- It documents a **traditional Chinese medicine external therapy** (Qimai Medicine's 54-card meridian conditioning system) combined with modern oncology treatment
- It presents a **novel mechanism hypothesis** ("liquefaction detoxification") that bridges traditional Chinese medicine theory ("expelling pathogenic toxins outward") with modern tumor microenvironment concepts
- It includes **long-term follow-up data** (555 sessions over 27 months) with objective biomarker and imaging evidence
- It addresses an important gap in the literature: the lack of systematic documentation of traditional Chinese medicine external therapies for locally advanced breast cancer with skin ulceration

## 2. Highlights（论文亮点，3-5条）

**Highlight 1**  
A 65-year-old breast cancer patient with liver metastasis received 555 sessions of Qimai Medicine over 27 months, combined with standard endocrine and targeted therapy.

**Highlight 2**  
Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA) normalized from 11.3 ng/mL to 2.05-2.16 ng/mL, and MRI showed liver metastases became "indistinct" or "no longer visible."

**Highlight 3**  
A unique "liquefaction detoxification" phenomenon was observed: umbilical yellow fluid discharge (9 months), tumor site ulcerative drainage, and hand desquamation.

**Highlight 4**  
The "pseudo-deterioration phase"—temporary worsening before improvement—represents a critical teaching point in long-term traditional Chinese medicine oncology therapy.

**Highlight 5**  
This case provides preliminary evidence that Qimai Medicine's external meridian conditioning may enhance tumor microenvironment remodeling when combined with standard oncological treatment.
